# The Speed Blur

## 1. A Radial Blur at Speed, Each Camera on Its Own Part of the Picture (2026-11-23)

**Date:** 2026-11-23. **Branch:** `claude/speed-blur`, from `main` at `f975ab8` (v0.0.27).

### 1.1 User Requests
- "¿Qué impacto tendría desenfocar la carretera por donde ha pasado la moto?" (what would blurring the road the bike has just ridden over cost?). Weighed: Bevy's motion blur needs a prepass of motion vectors and depth, drawing every mesh once more, on a base M4 Mac mini already bound by its geometry (−10 to −20 % estimated); Bevy's depth of field blurs by distance, not by motion; a radial blur of the picture, from where the road vanishes, needs no prepass (a fraction of a millisecond at 1080p).
- "vale, haz el desenfoque radial después del PR".

### 1.2 Implementation Details
- **`src/camera/speed_blur.rs`** (a module of `camera`, to read the private `ChaseCamera`) and **`src/camera/speed_blur.wgsl`**:
  - `SpeedBlur`, a component every chase camera requires (`#[require]` on `ChaseCamera`), is also the shader's uniform (`ShaderType`, 48 bytes, a whole number of 16 for WebGL): the camera's viewport in pixels, where the road ahead vanishes and where the bike is (across and down the viewport), the strength, the viewport's aspect.
  - `aim_speed_blur` (PostUpdate, after the transforms and the cameras are updated) aims it every frame from what `follow_player` now keeps in `ChaseCamera` (`speed` as a share of the top speed, `bike`, `ahead`: the way the camera looks, level with the bike's road): the strength `blur_at(speed)`, none up to 45 % of the top speed then a smoothstep to 1 at it; the vanishing point and the bike (0.8 m over its origin) through `Camera::world_to_ndc`. None but in `GameState::Race` with the setting on.
  - Extracted only while it blurs (`ExtractComponent` returning `None` at 0), so no pass is drawn otherwise; `UniformComponentPlugin` puts each camera's at an offset of one buffer.
  - The pass is the engine's own, drawn as Bevy's FXAA is (a fullscreen triangle, `ViewTarget::post_process_write`), in `Core3dSystems::PostProcess` after FXAA and SMAA, its pipeline specialized by the target's format. It was first written as a Bevy `FullscreenMaterial`: with two players the game stopped ("Dynamic binding offset ... would overrun the buffer"), as `FullscreenMaterial` keeps each view's bind groups while its textures last, and when a second camera made the uniform buffer grow it still bound the old one. The engine's pass makes its bind group every frame, with the buffer as it is.
  - The shader: outside the camera's viewport the picture is copied as it is (both cameras of a split screen draw into one picture, each pass over all of it); inside, ten samples from the pixel towards the vanishing point, reaching at most 12 % of the way there, weighted by how far out it is (from 0.18 to 0.75 of the viewport's height) and by how far from the bike (sharp within 0.08, as the rest past 0.24).
- **The setting** `Settings::speed_blur` (on by default; files without it read it as on), in Options > Graphics under the water ("Desenfoque de velocidad" / "Speed blur"), reset with the rest of Graphics; in the performance log (`speed_blur=`).
- **The benchmark**: `Scenario::speed_blur` (on in the base) and two scenarios, `1p-no-speed-blur` and `2p-no-speed-blur`: 18 a lap with one player and 6 with two.

### 1.3 Verification & Testing
- New tests: `the_blur_grows_smoothly_from_well_over_half_speed_to_the_top`, `points_go_across_and_down_the_viewport`, `the_shader_takes_whole_lines_of_sixteen_bytes`, `it_blurs_only_racing_fast_with_the_setting_on` (an app with a camera at 1920 × 1080 behind a bike: full strength at the top speed, the vanishing point over the bike and both in the middle across; none at 30 % of the top speed, with the setting off, or out of a race). The options' and the bench's tests take the new item and scenarios.
- `cargo test` (696 passed), `cargo fmt --all --check`, `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` clean.
- Seen on the M4 mini, one player on a game's circuit at 181 and 232 km/h: the bike sharp, the road under it and the sides streaked towards the vanishing point, the HUD untouched (drawn by its own camera). With two players the benchmark rode its lap through all its settings with no render error, after the fix; not yet seen nor measured (the mini was in use: in the background macOS holds the game at 60 fps).

## 4. What Stands on the Road Stays Sharp (2026-11-24)

**Branch:** `claude/blur-keeps-bikes-sharp`, from `main` at `d8a10a5` (v0.0.30, which released section 3 through PR #13).

### 4.1 User Request
- "observando la demo me he dado cuenta de un error, las motos que corren al lado o un poco detrás, no deben verse borrosas, ya que comparten velocidad con la moto del jugador": the bikes riding beside the player's, or a little behind, were blurred with the road under them in the picture.

### 4.2 Implementation Details
- The pass now reads the depth the camera drew the picture with (`Camera3d::depth_texture_usages` given `TEXTURE_BINDING` as a chase camera gets its `SpeedBlur`, `read_depth`; bound as `ViewDepthTexture`, multisampled or not as the view's `Msaa`, a pipeline and a layout for each, `Depth`): no prepass, nothing drawn twice.
- `SpeedBlur` (128 bytes now) also carries `world_from_clip` (the camera's transform times its projection's inverse) and `road`, the plane of the road under the bike (`road_plane`): through the bike's origin, square to `ChaseCamera::road_up`, which `follow_player` works out from the bike's heading up or down its slope, level across it (`road_up`; the camera's own up round a loop or a tube), so the bike's lean does not tilt it.
- `speed_blur.wgsl`: `off_road` takes each pixel back to the world from its depth and measures how high over that plane it is: up to 0.15 m it is road, from 0.35 m it stands on it (the sky too). A pixel standing on the road is not blurred; the samples of a road pixel are weighted by how much each is road, so a bike is not smeared over the road round it either.
- On the web (WebGL 2 cannot read a multisampled depth) the pass reads none and blurs as before (`READS_DEPTH`).

### 4.3 Verification & Testing
- New tests: `it_tells_the_road_from_what_stands_on_it_by_the_depth` (the shader's sums mirrored: the rider drawn where it is kept sharp, 0.8 m over the road; the road beside and behind the bike, under it in the picture, at 0 m, and a point 0.6 m over it at 0.6 m), `up_off_the_road_is_square_to_its_slope_and_level_across_it`; the uniform's size 128.
- `cargo test` (700 passed), `cargo fmt --all --check`, `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` clean.
- Seen on the M4 mini with two players on a game's circuit at 200–244 km/h, the screen split and MSAA 4×, at 70 % and 100 %: no render error, the road under each bike streaked as before, each bike sharp.

## 5. The Rider's Own Bike Sharp, Down to Its Tyres (2026-11-24)

**Branch:** `claude/blur-keeps-bikes-sharp`, after section 4's commit.

### 5.1 User Request
- "no debe desenfocarse la propia moto del jugador": the player's own bike must never blur. With section 4 alone its tyres, within 0.15 m of the road, could; the screen's sharp ring round the bike (`KEEP`) left a disc of sharp road round it instead.

### 5.2 Implementation Details
- `SpeedBlur::bike` (the uniform is 144 bytes now): the middle of the bike and its rider, 0.6 m up off the road over the bike's origin (`BIKE_MIDDLE`; the origin is on the road between the wheels).
- `speed_blur.wgsl`: within 1.2 m of that middle (fading out by 1.5 m: its wheels, tail and rider, leaning or not), what is more than 0.03–0.08 m over the road already stands on it, against 0.15–0.35 m elsewhere: the tyres stay sharp down to where they touch the road, and the road round them still blurs.
- With the depth read, the screen's ring round the bike is gone (the depth tells the bike from the road), so the road blurs right up to the bike; where the depth is not read (the web) the ring stays.
- A shader variable named `from` failed to compile (a reserved word in WGSL; Bevy then draws no blur, and logs it): named `low` and `high`.

### 5.3 Verification & Testing
- Seen on the M4 mini in a race on a game's circuit with the player's settings (SMAA, so the depth of one sample a pixel), the blur forced to its most from the grid in a scratch build (not committed): the rider's own bike and the rival on the grid beside it sharp, tyres included; the road blurred up to the bike, with no sharp disc round it; no render error.
- `cargo test` (700 passed), `cargo fmt --all --check`, `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` clean.
- Seen in passing: the game sometimes logs Bevy's `slab_allocator` "Use-after-free: attempted to copy element data for an unallocated key" in the menus, a couple of seconds after it starts (in two launches of four, never in a race nor in the benchmark); unrelated to this, to look at apart.
